# Demo walkthrough — following the platform, screen by screen

**Who this is for:** anyone showing The Deal Room to a private equity audience — partners,
an investment committee, a deal team. It assumes you know deals and assumes nothing
about software.

**How long:** 25 minutes at a walking pace, or 12 if you do Acts 1, 4 and 7 only.

**Before you present it once,** sit with this document open beside the live product and
click through it. Every screen name, tile and button below has been checked against the
product — but a demo you have not driven yourself is a demo that will surprise you.

> Every claim in this script has been verified on screen. If you find one that has not
> aged well, correct it here rather than working around it live.

---

## Before you start

- Open the **Deal Room** — either the tab inside Microsoft Teams or the same thing in a
  browser. They are identical.
- Give the first screen about **fifteen seconds** to fill in. It is loading the deals.
- **Say this once, out loud, at the start.** It saves you three awkward moments later:

  > "Everything you see today is a demonstration book — invented companies, invented
  > people, invented numbers, so nothing confidential is on screen. The market and news
  > panels are running on demonstration data too. The product connects to live public
  > filings and news, and you will see those sources listed under Settings, but nothing
  > on screen today is coming from outside."

- There is a **grey bar** under the top bar reading *"Showcase mode — viewing as
  partner…"*. Read it aloud once. It tells the room this is a demonstration **and** that
  the access rules on it are real anyway.
- The people in the top-bar dropdown are **placeholder names** — Eleanor Shellstrop,
  Chidi Anagonye, and so on. Say so once and nobody will ask again.

## How the window is laid out

Three bands, and they never move.

**The top bar** — the product name, then, left to right:

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| The **person dropdown** | Who you are signed in as. Fourteen placeholder people for the demo. |
| **+ New deal** | Enter a deal you already have, without going through screening. |
| **💬 Ask the assistant** | Opens the assistant on whatever you are looking at. |
| **☀** | Light or dark. |
| **⚙** (Settings) | Which outside data sources the product is allowed to use. |

**The five main tabs** — this is the whole product. There is nothing else to find.

| Tab | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| **Home** | What needs you today. |
| **Sourcing & screening** | Companies you are looking at but have not committed to. |
| **Deals in flight** | The deals you are actually running. |
| **Fund & Portfolio** | The fund's money, and the companies it already owns. |
| **Report** | The numbers you would send an investor. |

**The page itself**, below the tabs.

That is the entire navigation. Five tabs, and one more level when you open a deal. If
you get lost, press a main tab and you are back somewhere known.

### Two badges you will be asked about

| Badge | What it means |
|---|---|
| **▤ Composed** | Assembled from the record by fixed rules. **No AI wrote a word of it**, and it cannot change a deal's status. |
| **✦ AI** | A model wrote the prose, from that deal's own record, with its sources listed. |

Home's daily briefing is **Composed**. The brief inside a deal is **AI**. Say which is
which before anyone asks — it is the first question every committee asks.

## Act 1 · Home — *what needs you today* ⏱ 4 min

Press **Home**. Read it top to bottom; it is built to be read that way. There are eight
panels and they arrive in this order.

**1. Daily briefing** *(▤ Composed)*. A written account of the fund's position, in
sentences, for whoever is signed in. A partner and an analyst get different briefings
from the same record. Press the **🔍 Evidence** button in the panel header — every
numbered claim expands to the deal, workstream or filing it came from.

> "The first thing you see is not a chart. It is somebody telling you what happened —
> and every sentence will show you its working."

**2. Untracked follow-ups** *(▤ Composed)* — **the panel people remember.** Somebody
promised something in a Teams channel and nobody wrote it down. The product found 39
of them across 19 deals, each quoted verbatim with who said it and by when.

> "It does not chase these for you. It tells you they exist. Every firm in this room
> has a version of this problem and no way of seeing it."

**3. What needs my attention.** The queue. Each row says which deal, what is wrong, and
gives you a button to the place where you would fix it.

**4. The four figures.** For a partner or committee chair these are: **Ready for IC**,
**Not IC-ready**, **Deals with conditions open**, and **Next IC**. Each has a line
underneath saying exactly what it counts.

> "Those are the four things a committee chair actually asks on a Monday. Note that
> 'conditions open' is separate — those deals are already approved; something was
> attached to the approval and has not been cleared."

*(A more junior person sees different figures here, chosen for their job. You will see
that in Act 7.)*

**5. ⚙ Hide sections I don't use.** Next to *"Showing all 8 sections"*. Every panel on
this page can be switched off, per person.

**6. Deals by stage.** Four blocks — Origination & Screening, Diligence & Approval,
Execution & Closing, Value & Exit. The header above them gives the total and the
pre-completion split: *"$8.1B across 19 deals · $6.4B of it pre-completion."* The blocks
add up to exactly that. Press one to see those deals.

**7. Origination funnel.** How many were sourced, screened, shortlisted, and how many
are still in origination. Read the note underneath — it explains that this is not a
screening yield, because targets you passed on never entered the record.

**8. Pipeline deals** — all 19 in one list, with a **Compare** control: tick two to four
and put them side by side on the same decision fields.

**9. Market intelligence** — comparables and precedents. **On the demonstration book
this is empty.** Say: *"That is the panel that fills from your own past deals. This
book has no history behind it."*

**→ Next:** press **Sourcing & screening**.

## Act 2 · Sourcing & screening — *before it is a deal* ⏱ 4 min

Everything you look at *before* the fund commits. Four sub-tabs across the top:
**Pipeline · Sourcing framework · Analyst research · Emails & news**.

### Pipeline

- **Deals in origination** at the top — four real cards (Mojave Renewables, Harborlight
  Marine Services, Cypress Grove Dental Partners, Riverbend Specialty Foods), each with
  size, sector, which of the sixteen steps it has reached, and how ready it is for
  committee. **Point at these.** Any one opens.
- **The funnel** below counts where deals have got to. **Read it out; do not click it** —
  it is a counter, not a filter.
- **Candidate pipeline** below that is empty on the demonstration book. Say: *"That fills
  from the news and filings screen — you will see how in a moment."*

### Sourcing framework — *the part worth slowing down for*

This is the piece that tells a private equity audience the product was built by someone
who has done the job. Two panels:

- **Fund mandate — a hard box.** The binding limits in the fund's own agreement with its
  investors: fund size, enterprise value range, geographies. A company outside the
  mandate is **excluded, never scored**. You cannot buy it, so there is no point ranking
  it. The screen calls it *"the hard box every target must fit"*.
- **Investment themes — a guide.** Three of them, each owned by a named partner, each
  with a thesis and a *"why now"*. A partner's hunting ground. Shapes what you look for;
  excludes nothing.

  > "A mandate and a theme are not the same thing and confusing them is how a fund
  > wastes a quarter on something it was never permitted to buy. One is a rule from your
  > investors. The other is a partner's judgement."

  *Scored candidate rankings appear beneath these once targets are in the pipeline. On
  the demonstration book there are none — describe it, do not point at it.*

### Analyst research and Emails & news

Where broker material and public filings arrive, and where a company becomes a
candidate. **Both are empty on the demonstration book.** Show the shape of them —
*"filings and press on the companies you are watching, and a Screen → button on each to
push one into the pipeline"* — and move on.

**→ Next:** press **Deals in flight**.

---

## Act 3 · Deals in flight — *the list* ⏱ 2 min

One row per deal: whether it is IC-ready, the company, where it is in the sixteen steps,
what is holding it up, the size, and the committee date.

**Say the number difference before anyone asks.** The header reads *"15 of 15 · 4 more
still in screening — see Sourcing & screening."* Home says 19 because Home counts
everything; this tab shows only deals that have actually launched.

Across the top are five filters with counts: **All · Needs attention · Diligence ·
Execution · Value & Exit**.

Press any row to open a deal.

> "It takes about ten seconds. It is assembling the whole deal — the workstreams, the
> committee papers, the documents and the audit trail."

**→ Next:** open **Helvetia Diagnostics**.

## Act 4 · Inside a deal — *the tabs across the top* ⏱ 8 min

The heart of the product, and where a first-time user needs the most help. Say this
before you click anything:

> "A deal has nine tabs, in the same order on every deal, always. Left to right: what
> happened, what we think, are we ready, what does it return, what is outstanding, do
> the work, the diligence detail, the paperwork — and everything else under More."

| Tab | What is on it |
|---|---|
| **Deal brief** | A written account of where this deal stands and what to do next. Start here. |
| **Thesis & key figures** | The investment case, the headline numbers, and where each figure came from. |
| **IC readiness** | Whether the committee papers are complete, and what is missing. **This tab is the record.** |
| **Returns, plan & risk** | The returns model, sources and uses, the EBITDA bridge, the risk register, the IOI and the LOI. |
| **Progress & follow-ups** | Steps completed, who moved the deal on, and what people have promised. |
| **Work the deal** | The sixteen steps of the lifecycle. Run a step, or move the deal on. |
| **Diligence workstreams** | Financial, Legal, Tax, Commercial, ESG and the rest — status, owner, findings. The **data room** is at the bottom of this tab. |
| **Documents** | Generate a committee memo or deck from the record. |
| **More ▾** | Deal channel, Comparables & precedents, Generate a document, Audit trail. |

Four things to show, in this order.

### 1. It tells you where to start

**Above the tabs**, before the brief begins, is a one-line instruction: *"Where to
start · Drive to close · Approved at IC — advance execution and closing"*, with a button
**Go to Work the deal ▸**. Read it, then press it, then come back.

Below it is the brief itself *(✦ AI)*: prose, not a dashboard, ending with **Ask next** —
four questions written for this deal that you can press instead of typing.

### 2. The numbers agree with each other

Open **Thesis & key figures**. The IC readiness card says *"2 obligations still
outstanding"* and then lists exactly two. Hover any key figure to see its source —
*"source: QoE final · high confidence"*.

Now open **Diligence workstreams**. The header states the position honestly: **"4 closed
at IC, no write-up"**, and each of those rows says *"Closed at IC — no write-up on
file"*.

> "That is not a fault the software is hiding. It is telling a partner that four
> workstreams were signed off at committee with nothing written up afterwards. That is
> a records gap, not outstanding work — and knowing the difference is the whole job."

Scroll to the bottom of this tab for the **data room**: fourteen numbered folders, the
named adviser on each workstream, playbook templates. **This is not a copy of your data
room — it is your data room, in SharePoint, opened from here.**

### 3. The assistant drafts; the tab is the record

Press **💬 Ask the assistant** and ask about this deal — *"what is outstanding before we
can close?"*

Note the **Focus** box at the top of the chat. It is set to this deal, so answers come
from this deal only; ask about another and it will decline. You can change Focus to a
different deal, or to everything you are allowed to see. The point is not that it cannot
roam — it is that **it only ever roams inside what your role permits**.

> "Everything it says is drawn from this deal's record and it will show you its sources.
> But the record of what is outstanding is the **IC readiness** tab. The assistant
> drafts; the tab decides. Nothing goes into a committee paper without being checked
> against it."

Say that line whether or not the answer looks right. It is the honest position, and a
room that hears you say it will trust the rest of the demo more, not less.

**→ Next:** press **Fund & Portfolio**.

## Act 5 · Fund & Portfolio — *the money* ⏱ 4 min

**The fund.** Committed capital, invested, dry powder, and the returns — TVPI, DPI,
RVPI, gross and net MOIC, gross and net IRR, total value. Each carries a note on how it
was worked out.

**Portfolio monitoring.** The companies the fund already owns: hold period, entry versus
current valuation, EBITDA, MOIC, IRR, progress through the value-creation plan, status.

Point at the line beneath the panel header. It names any deal that has completed but is
not yet in portfolio reporting, and says its value is not in the figures above — with a
button to open it.

> "Six companies here and three completed deals under Deals in flight is a difference
> somebody will spot. So the product tells you the answer instead of leaving you to
> work it out."

**The watchlist.** The names going the wrong way, each with the one KPI that is off
plan.

**Concentration against LPA limits.** Sector and single-position exposure against the
caps in your own fund agreement.

> "That is the panel that ends a conversation with an LP before it starts."

**→ Next:** press **Report**.

---

## Act 6 · Report — *what you would send an investor* ⏱ 2 min

Every figure with its source, its as-of date and how it was worked out, in one place.

It arrives marked **"Draft — not certified"**, and there is a **✓ Certify for LP use**
button.

> "Nothing reaches an investor until a named person presses that button, and pressing it
> freezes a dated copy that cannot then be edited. Until then it says draft, on every
> page, in writing."

The header line also states **"Market data: seeded"** — the product declaring, on the
document you would send out, which of its inputs are not live. Point at it. It is the
single most credible thing on the screen.

**→ Next:** the person dropdown, top left of the buttons.

---

## Act 7 · Different people, different views ⏱ 4 min

**This is the strongest act in the demo. Do not cut it.**

Open the person dropdown and choose **Chidi Anagonye — Analyst — Northeast coverage**.

Watch what happens:

- **Deals in flight** drops from 15 to 4.
- A deal they are not cleared for still appears — *"Status only · Beacon Hill Biotech 🔒
  · You are not on this deal team"* — by name and status, with no valuation, no
  diligence and no documents. It is honest that it exists rather than pretending it does
  not.
- Look at the filters: **"Value & Exit 0"** is greyed out rather than hidden. The product
  tells the analyst the filter exists and the answer for them is nothing.
- The daily briefing is rewritten for their job, and **the four figures on Home change
  to different figures**, chosen for what an analyst actually does.
- Ask the assistant about a deal they cannot open and it will not discuss it.

> "This is not a display setting. The restricted deal never reaches the screen — it is
> not hidden in the page, it is not sent to the browser. There is nothing to inspect and
> nothing to switch back on."

Switch back to the partner before you continue.

## Act 8 · Where the data comes from ⏱ 2 min

Press **⚙ Settings**. It lists every outside source the product may use — SEC EDGAR
(company filings), news, company-identity registries, web search, and the paid market
providers if your firm subscribes — and **you can turn any of them off**.

> "You decide what this thing is allowed to look at. And the deal material itself never
> leaves your own Microsoft tenant — the data room is your SharePoint, the conversation
> is your Teams channel, the mail and calendar are your own."

Close with:

> "One place to run a deal, built on the tools you already pay for and already trust,
> and every number on it will tell you where it came from."

## The questions you will actually be asked

Have an answer ready. Say "I will come back to you" rather than guessing — but do not be
caught without any of these.

| Question | The honest answer |
|---|---|
| **Where does our data live?** | In your own Microsoft 365 tenant. The data room is your SharePoint, the conversations are your Teams channels. The product reads them with the permissions you grant and stores its own deal record alongside. |
| **Does the AI read our confidential documents?** | Only what the person asking is already entitled to open — the same permission check runs first. |
| **Does anything leave the firm?** | Deal material stays in your tenant. The outside sources under Settings are one-way, public and individually switchable. |
| **What if the assistant is wrong?** | It drafts; it never decides. The IC readiness tab is the record and nothing reaches a committee paper unchecked. |
| **Who can see what?** | Act 7. Show it rather than describing it. |
| **What does it cost?** | Get a current number from the delivery team before the meeting. Do not say "almost nothing". |
| **How long to set up?** | Get a current number. It needs a Microsoft 365 tenant and an administrator to approve access. |
| **Does it work on my phone?** | It runs in Teams and in a browser. Check it yourself on the device you will be asked about. |
| **Does it connect to our CRM?** | Ask the delivery team what exists today. Do not improvise. |
| **When do we see our own deals?** | The demonstration book is separate. Loading a firm's own deals is a piece of work with a timeline — get it before the meeting. |

## If something goes wrong

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| A deal takes ten seconds to open | Expected — it is assembling the full record. Keep talking. |
| A panel is empty (candidate pipeline, ranked targets, analyst research, news, comparables, IC precedents) | Expected on the demonstration book. Say what fills it and move on. Never click around hunting for content. |
| The funnel does not respond | It is a counter, not a filter. Read it. |
| An answer looks wrong | Say the line from Act 4: the assistant drafts, the IC readiness tab is the record. Then show the tab. This is a better moment than a smooth one. |
| You are lost | Press a main tab. There are only five and they always work. |

---

## The one-paragraph version

> The Deal Room is where a deal team runs the whole arc in one place — find a company,
> screen it against the rules the fund is bound by, run the diligence, take it to
> committee, close it, and then monitor the company they now own. It runs inside Teams
> or a browser. Every answer comes from the fund's own record and shows its working.
> Each person sees only what their role permits, and the product will tell you when
> there is something it is not showing you.
