Why this is the change worth making

  • Navigation modelled the process, not the person. Four of seven tabs were a stage. Nobody starts their day wanting to visit Stage 3 — they want to know what moved, what is stuck, and what needs them.
  • Deals moved house when they progressed. Under a stage-first nav, a deal you are responsible for relocates to a different tab when it advances. A deal should stay where you left it.
  • The landing page led with capability, not with work. The agent catalogue — itself organised by stage — owned the top of the screen, and “what needs my attention” sat below the fold in third place.
  • Almost nothing is new. The queue, briefing, commitments and KPIs already exist and already work; this mostly reorders them. Only What changed and My deals are net-new, and both are built from data already in the record.
  • Stages do not disappear. They become a filter on Deals, a chip on every row, and the progress rail inside a deal — which is the one place where “where are we and what is next” is genuinely the question being asked.
