Why this is the change worth making

  • Navigation modelled the process, not the person. Four of seven tabs were a stage. Nobody starts their day wanting to visit Stage 3 — they want to know what moved, what is stuck, and what needs them.
  • Deals moved house when they progressed. A deal you are responsible for relocated to a different tab when it advanced. A deal should stay where you left it.
  • The landing page led with capability, not with work. The agent catalogue — itself organised by stage — owned the top of the screen while “what needs my attention” sat third, below the fold.
  • Stages 2–4 were already a filter. They are the same component three times — filter the deal list on a stage prefix, render a grid, append a guide. Collapsing them removes roughly 215 lines of duplication and no capability.
  • Stage 1 was never the same kind of thing. It works on candidates rather than deals, and one of its actions creates a deal. It becomes Sourcing — a desk, not a stage — which fixes the stage-as-address problem without pretending a funnel is a filtered list.
  • Almost none of this is new. Home is today's dashboard reordered, with the briefing collapsed and the catalogue moved. The only net-new build is the change digest, and it ships scoped to what the record already holds.

What this proposal does not fix

  • The deal interior is still twelve tabs. Cockpit, workflow, threads, documents, overview, stages, workspace, research, artifacts, IC, activity. If the brief is “focus on the day-to-day”, this is where the day is actually spent — and it is the more overloaded surface by a wide margin. It should be the next cycle.
  • Push versus pull. “What changed” may be a notification problem wearing a page section's clothes. The proactive bot card is arguably the right home for the channel-traffic half of it.
  • Fund & Portfolio still overlaps the Value & Exit filter. Both list owned companies. Collapsing four stage tabs on a duplication argument while leaving that overlap is inconsistent — it needs a deliberate answer, not an assertion that fund-level is “a different question”.
  • Narrow width. This is a Teams tab. KPI row plus two columns plus a six-item queue plus a change feed is a long scroll in a sidebar-width surface, and the mockup only reflows at 1150px.
  • Region scope. Two personas are scoped to a region. Both the deal list and the change digest have to honour it; the mockup shows the selector but not the behaviour.

Why this is a tab and not a filter

revised
Rev 1 folded Stage 1 into the Deals stage filter alongside Stages 2–4. That was wrong, and it was the largest hole in the proposal. Stages 2–4 render deals; Stage 1 renders candidates, which are a different population that isn't in the deal list at all — and one of its actions creates a deal. Filtering Deals to “Origination” would have shown four ordinary deal rows while the funnel a sourcing analyst actually lives in had nowhere to go.
So it keeps its own tab and takes its own name. Sourcing is a desk, not a stage of a deal — which means renaming it removes the stage-as-address problem outright rather than working around it.
