Cut from this proposal

Rev 3 sketched a second Home for the operating partner — overdue reporting packs, covenant headroom, levers behind plan. It has been removed rather than refined, for two reasons given by two different reviewers.

First, it was scope creep on a release whose users are the deal team, and the cost of making one shell serve two very different jobs lands on the deal-team Home.

Second, and worse: the cards used the six real portfolio companies from app/data/portfolio.js with figures that are not in the record. Vertex Data is status: 'on-track' there and “pack overdue, third month running” here; Harbor’s pricing lever is progressPct: 44 there and “30% against 60% plan” here. Real names carrying invented facts is how a mockup becomes a screenshot and then a commitment.

The underlying point stands and is recorded in the appendix: portfolio operations has no home in this product, and the first thing it needs is a reporting-cycle entity — the only one of the three asks that generates time-stamped facts for the other two to hang off.

Rationale, scope and what this does not fix

Navigation

HomeSourcingDealsFund & Portfolio
  • Four of seven tabs were a stage. Stage is something a deal has, not somewhere a person goes — so a deal relocated to a different tab when it advanced.
  • Stages 2–4 become a filter on one Deals list. They are three small components that all filter the same array and render a card grid; the card content they each add moves into the row and the stage-tools panel.
  • Stage 1 becomes Sourcing and keeps its tab. It operates on candidates rather than deals and one of its actions creates a deal. It also already lists origination-stage deals, so those stay there and are excluded from Deals — a deal appears on exactly one list.
  • Report leaves the tab bar. It keeps its existing ?view=report route and its pinned channel tab.
  • The agent catalogue moves out of the landing page into the agents panel. The access strip it contained stays on Home in its own right.

IC readiness: verdict, not percentage — now wired, not drawn

  • The percentage is 45% average lane progress, 35% memo section status, 20% compliance. Lane progress is typed by hand and a draft memo section scores 0.6, so the number is gameable and everyone will learn how within a month.
  • Rev 3 drew this fix without making it. assess() in homeDesk.js did not import icReadiness.js at all — every branch still tested num(deal.readiness), and the gating sentences on the screen (“QoE, legal report, tax structuring paper”) were strings the function cannot emit. That is now done properly. assess(deal, raw) ranks on verdict.state and renders verdict.gating verbatim.
  • Four things had to change in the engine before the verdict meant anything:
    • The verdict had one reachable state. The artifact check tested deal.artifacts.D1/D2/D3 — the AI generator’s cache. No seeded deal has it, so gating was never empty and CONDITIONAL and READY were both unreachable. Every one of the 15 rows would have read “Not IC-ready”.
    • The check asked the wrong question. “Has the agent been run?” is not “does the paper exist?”. A deal.icPapers record satisfies it by any route, and diligence papers accumulate with the step — you cannot stand at D3 (draft the memo) with no plan and no findings behind you.
    • Any lane under 80% blocked. So nothing mid-diligence could ever clear, and it handed the analyst who types the number a switch that silently flipped the verdict. A lane now blocks when it has not opened, is halted, or carries an unresolved high-severity finding.
    • Gating was a bare count. “3 required artifact(s) incomplete” sends the reader into the deal to find out which — the exact click this surface exists to save. It names them, and the labels lost their D1/D2 codes.
  • Result against the seeded record: NOT-READY 13, READY 4, CONDITIONAL 2, with gating that differs by step rather than one sentence repeated 19 times.
  • Progress bars come off the rows. A bar invites comparison between two numbers that were typed by different analysts.
  • Honest cost note: this was not “no new scoring, no new data”, as rev 3 claimed. The verdict needs the unredacted record, so the queue, the KPI row and three briefing sentences all had to route through the access-checked resolver.

Step position

  • Diligence · step 2 of 5 uses stageStepNumber / stageStepTotal, which the record already emits. An earlier draft used a global n/16; that spine puts Archive mid-way and parks a healthy owned company at 14/16, so the denominator was misleading.
  • Fixed here: a screened deal was given position 0 of the five diligence steps, rendering “step 0 of 5” for a deal that has never been in diligence. Both fields are null for a screened deal now, so a consumer shows a real position or shows nothing.
  • Still true and not fixed: D5 is Archive, so “step 4 of 5” inside Diligence still counts a post-decision step in its denominator. Correcting that means re-cutting the stage spine in flow.js, which is a bigger change than this release.

Access

  • Two defects were found while preparing this and are fixed in the same change. The cross-deal commitments feed read the unredacted record for every visible deal, including status-tier deals the reader is not on — surfacing named people and verbatim channel quotes. And every deal summary shipped per-lane diligence status to status-tier seats.
  • Both fixes were then reviewed and both were incomplete. The commitments guard failed open when accessLevel was absent — and absent is not a trusted internal path, because listAgentDeals stamps 'full' on everything it returns with no identity argument at all. It is inverted to refuse anything that is not explicitly 'full'.
  • Stripping the lane array did not close the exposure either: diligenceProgress is the mean of that array, and the memo and compliance counts are the same substance one aggregate removed. All are nulled at the tier boundary. readiness survives deliberately as the single overall figure, and the card on Home says exactly that.
  • A regression was introduced and fixed: with the lane array emptied, the attention queue fell through every lane branch and landed on “On track” — a health claim about a deal whose health data the reader had just been refused. Metadata-tier deals get their own row. There is a test.
  • Not fixed here, and named: the /fund/* routes discard the caller's identity entirely. And 'full' is broader than it sounds — RESTRICTED_STAGE_RE only covers D/E/V stages, so an origination deal is full for any in-region user, and the commitments feed will mine its channel. That is policy-consistent but has never been policy-reviewed.
  • Production, not beta. recordEvent has no actor field, is not append-only, swallows failures and captures only writes — so “who saw what, when” is unanswerable, which means the leak fixed above cannot be investigated retrospectively. Channel-utterance monitoring has had no DPIA and no works-council consultation. Both are hard stops before a live tenant, neither blocks a seeded demo tenant.

What changed since you were last here

  • Ships from the deal activity log and data-room changes, filtered by a watermark held server-side per user and routed through the same access check as the deal list.
  • That sentence is not sufficient on its own, and the implementation must also: floor the watermark at the user’s join timestamp per deal, not their last visit — otherwise adding someone to a team hands them the deal’s entire prior activity, which is retrospective wall-crossing with no crossing record; filter events by the access level at event time; re-run the tier strip on every event body, since events carry lane and channel content the list now removes; exclude status-tier deals rather than degrading them; and write a read event per digest.
  • Deferred, with reasons: readiness movements (computed live, no time series to diff), IC-date changes (nothing writes an event when the date is edited), and channel volume (a slow per-deal Graph call that belongs in a proactive card, not a page load).

Role-composed Home — withdrawn

  • Rev 3 proposed one shell and two payloads, on the grounds that “the route already takes a role and an operating-partner lens already exists”. Both claims were wrong and are withdrawn.
  • buildHomeDesk’s role is echoed back and never branched on, and it is filled from access.role — an RBAC tier (admin / partner / deal-team / analyst / member). Operating Partner is a function, not a tier: the parameter that exists is on the wrong axis.
  • personaLens is a prompt string prepended to an agent input. It shapes what a model says. It composes nothing.
  • So a second Home is a new builder on a new axis over data that does not exist — not a payload swap. It is cut from this release. What portfolio operations needs first is a reporting-cycle entity with expected submissions, due dates and received / late / missing status: the only one of its three gaps that generates time-stamped facts, which the dated KPI and the covenant test date then hang off.

Deliberately out of scope

  • Portfolio company as an entity created at completion, carrying the underwriting case, KPI definitions, IC conditions and post-close diligence findings across. Until it exists, Fund and Value & Exit stay two datasets.
  • Bolt-ons. A bolt-on is a deal with a parent; today it is an integer on the fund page. There is no slot for parentage in the IA and adding one is a data-model change.
  • Relationship intelligence from Graph. The M365 layer is provisioning only — no read of people, contacts or calendar. Answering “has anyone here ever met this company” is the largest unbuilt advantage of being Teams-native.
  • Intermediaries as objects — coverage owner, what they sent us, our response latency, our hit rate.
  • The deal interior. Twelve tabs. More overloaded than the top nav ever was, and the right target for the cycle after this one.
  • Process calendar, DD spend, financing workstream, team capacity — each is a real gap named by review and none is a navigation problem.
  • The exclusivity clock is a gap, but a fabricated one was worse. diligence.js invented the window as EV >= 500 ? 9 : 7 weeks and printed it next to real dates. Nothing on the record carries the exclusivity date agreed in the LOI, so it is now labelled as the planning assumption it is, everywhere it surfaces. Making it contractual needs a field that does not exist.
  • The handover from deal to portfolio. There is no moment where a closed deal becomes an owned company, and there cannot be until the company is an entity to hand it to. The post-close-tagged diligence findings that diligence.js already classifies, and which nothing consumes today, are the payload of that future handover — recorded here so it is not rediscovered.
